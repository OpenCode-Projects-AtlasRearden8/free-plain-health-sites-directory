# Free Plain-English Health Sites Directory

_Updated 2026-11-08_. Published by Asdale Dynamics, LLC. MIT licensed.

> This directory is education only. It is never medical advice. Nothing here diagnoses, treats, or replaces a conversation with your own clinician or pharmacist.
>
> **How ratings work:** reading levels are plain-language approximations —
> **Easy** (~5th–6th grade), **Moderate** (~8th–9th), **Advanced** (10th+).
> **Ad density** is what a reader actually sees around the content.

## Government & NIH health sites
Ad-free, public-domain teaching material from NIH, NLM, CDC, and USDA.


### MedlinePlus

- **Link:** https://medlineplus.gov/
- **Publisher:** U.S. National Library of Medicine (NIH)
- **Reading level:** Easy
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

The single best first stop for plain-English overviews of hundreds of health topics, drugs, supplements, and lab tests.

No ads, no account, no upsells. Written specifically for the general public, with Spanish versions and medical encyclopedia pages alongside.

---

### MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia

- **Link:** https://medlineplus.gov/encyclopedia.html
- **Publisher:** U.S. National Library of Medicine (NIH)
- **Reading level:** Easy
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

A-Z article-and-illustration encyclopedia covering tests, symptoms, injuries, and diseases in short, readable pieces.

The A.D.A.M. encyclopedia licensed for public use. Short entries, clear illustrations, handy when you want a two-minute answer.

---

### NIH News in Health

- **Link:** https://newsinhealth.nih.gov/
- **Publisher:** NIH Office of Communications
- **Reading level:** Easy
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

A free monthly newsletter translating new NIH-funded research into readable, friendly articles.

Great model of how to say 'what the research actually showed' without media spin. Print-friendly for clinic waiting rooms.

---

### National Institute on Aging (NIA)

- **Link:** https://www.nia.nih.gov/
- **Publisher:** NIH
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

Deep but readable guides on Alzheimer's, caregiving, medications and aging, fitness for older adults.

The Alzheimer's and caregiving sections are unusually kind in tone and come in large-print-style layouts.

---

### NIDDK (diabetes, digestive, kidney)

- **Link:** https://www.niddk.nih.gov/
- **Publisher:** NIH
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

Authoritative plain-language pages on diabetes, kidney disease, digestive conditions, and weight management.

Their diabetes series is written for real patients; Spanish versions available for most major topics.

---

### NHLBI (heart, lung, blood)

- **Link:** https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/
- **Publisher:** NIH
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

Heart-healthy eating plans (including DASH), blood pressure and cholesterol guides, sleep resources.

The DASH eating plan pages include printable handouts you can hand to a family member as-is.

---

### CDC (health topics A-Z)

- **Link:** https://www.cdc.gov/
- **Publisher:** U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

Not NIH, but the other big free government health library: vaccines, infections, chronic conditions, travel health.

Government agency pages are federal public-domain education material; no commercial ads. Some topic pages vary in reading level.

## Hospital teaching libraries
Clinician-reviewed article libraries from major hospitals.


### Cleveland Clinic Health Library

- **Link:** https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health
- **Publisher:** Cleveland Clinic
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** Low
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no
- **Flags:** sponsored content present

Thousands of reviewed articles on symptoms, diseases, tests, and treatments, written by clinicians in plain English.

Best-in-class hospital teaching site. Some sections include clearly labeled sponsored health content, which is why ad density is 'Low' rather than 'None'.

---

### Johns Hopkins Medicine Health Library

- **Link:** https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health
- **Publisher:** Johns Hopkins Medicine
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** Low
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

Condition and treatment articles plus renowned plain-English explainers on nutrition and 'healthy living' topics.

The 'Healthy Living' library (including their well-known sodium, sugar, and food-assignment explainers) is a standout.

---

### University of Michigan Health Library

- **Link:** https://www.uofmhealth.org/health-library
- **Publisher:** Michigan Medicine, University of Michigan
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

A large encyclopedia-style library of conditions, tests, medications, and self-care topics.

Quiet layout, essentially ad-free while reading; created with the Healthwise teaching library.

---

### Mount Sinai Health Library

- **Link:** https://www.mountsinai.org/health-library
- **Publisher:** Mount Sinai Health System
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

Categorized library of diseases, conditions, injuries, screenings, and drug information.

Includes alternative-medicine and herbal-supplement monographs that most hospital libraries skip.

---

### Mayo Clinic

- **Link:** https://www.mayoclinic.org/
- **Publisher:** Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research
- **Reading level:** Advanced
- **Ad density:** Moderate
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no
- **Flags:** high-value but busiest commercial layout

Very thorough disease-and-symptom articles; the 'Diseases & Conditions' A-Z is encyclopedic.

Content quality is excellent and editorially independent, but the reading level runs a notch above MedlinePlus and you'll see banner ads and sponsor boxes around the content. Good second opinion, not a first stop for easy reading.

## Free meal & medication trackers
Free tools for logging meals or meds. Account/privacy flags are called out explicitly.


### MyPlate.gov (USDA)

- **Link:** https://www.myplate.gov/
- **Publisher:** U.S. Department of Agriculture
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** None
- **Account required:** no
- **Collects personal data:** no

Free food-group guidance, printable MyPlate plans, recipes, and quizzes. No commercial ads at all.

Use the site fully without an account for guidance and printable plans. If you want the government tracker app, you create an account there — this directory points you to the reading site only.

---

### Medisafe (medication reminder)

- **Link:** https://www.medisafe.com/
- **Publisher:** Medisafe
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** Low
- **Account required:** yes
- **Collects personal data:** yes
- **Flags:** account required; collects personal data

Freemium app for med schedules, refill reminders, and pill-interaction checks.

The free tier covers the core reminder job. Requires an account and collects usage/personal data (their privacy policy says so plainly). Review the privacy settings before syncing with family members.

---

### MyFitnessPal (meal tracker)

- **Link:** https://www.myfitnesspal.com/
- **Publisher:** MyFitnessPal, Inc.
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** Moderate
- **Account required:** yes
- **Collects personal data:** yes
- **Flags:** account required; collects personal data; ads in free tier

The best-known free food and calorie tracker, with a huge community food database.

Free tier is genuinely usable for basic tracking. Expect account signup, ads in the free app, and substantial personal-data collection for its business model.

---

### Cronometer (meal/nutrition tracker)

- **Link:** https://cronometer.com/
- **Publisher:** Cronometer Software Inc.
- **Reading level:** Moderate
- **Ad density:** Low
- **Account required:** yes
- **Collects personal data:** yes
- **Flags:** account required; collects personal data

Free nutrition tracker that stands out for verified nutrient data instead of crowd-entered foods.

Free web version does the core job with fewer ads than rival free trackers. Account required; it stores diet data on their servers, so read the privacy policy.
